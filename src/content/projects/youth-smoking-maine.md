## Overview

With teammates Charlie and Noku, I analyzed Maine Integrated Youth Health Survey (MIYHS) data from 2009–2021 to understand how youth smoking behavior evolved in Androscoggin County, Maine, and where public health interventions were still falling short.

## Findings

- Smoking among 18-year-olds fell from over a quarter of respondents in 2009 to around 10% by 2019, tracking a broader decline across all age groups.
- A persistent gender gap remained even as both groups declined: 28.6% of 18+ males reported smoking in 2009 versus 23.1% of females, a gap that held through 2021.
- Racial and ethnic disparities were stark: in 2009, nearly 40% of Hispanic teens reported smoking compared to 15% of White teens, pointing to a need for more culturally responsive prevention programs.
- Maine's 2019 Tobacco 21 law coincided with continued declines, though the rise of e-cigarette marketing around 2014–2017 appears to have slowed cigarette-specific declines for some groups.
